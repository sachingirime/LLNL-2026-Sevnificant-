## Ranked shortlist and disproof experiment

1. **Tube-constrained connectivity** — run on the registered real mask, excluding plates/caps.  It fails if calls cluster monotonically with x (the measured registration drift) or if a purported disconnected strut has no internal empty run between material-bearing ends.
2. **EDT per-strut radius profile** — run on the same struts and mask.  It fails as a gross-missing comparator if its low-radius flags do not agree with low tube coverage; it is deliberately not promoted to a real-scan thin/dross detector.  Its thin/dross behavior must be checked on the labelled 17.8-µm unit cells before any future automatic use.
3. **Strut-aligned contour/trajectory review** — test on the six labelled simulated unit cells.  It fails if the defect named in a file cannot be localized in an aligned strut volume without relying on the filename.  It is retained for visual adjudication, not batch calls on the coarse real scan.

## Gap statement

For the registered 58.1-µm scan, only **missing** and sufficiently wide **disconnected** gaps are supportable automatic labels.  A real strut is roughly four voxels across and the EDT radius has only about seven useful quantized levels; therefore thin versus nominal and attached dross versus a locally misregistered/rough surface are below a defensible resolution margin.  Bent-strut estimation also needs stable cross-sectional centres, which this scan cannot guarantee.  Those three classes are appropriate for the 17.8-µm labelled simulated unit cells and for human review with a strut-aligned contour view, not for reported real-specimen rates.

## Addendum: 2-D transformed-space methods (2026-07-25)

The additional screen found methods that use **2-D views after moving each strut into its own local coordinate system**, not an axis-aligned CT slice.  This distinction matters: a global radiograph/projection superposes many octet members, whereas a registered local reformat preserves strut attribution.

| 2-D method | Published precedent | What it contributes here | Limitation / experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strut-aligned longitudinal projection / Contour View | Miao *et al.* [1] rotate each strut into a standard orientation and use a 2-D contour representation. | A CT mask can be maximum-projected through a narrow transverse local slab to form a longitudinal × transverse image; a gap is a dark longitudinal run. | A maximum projection may hide an out-of-plane gap behind nearby material.  Compare its calls with the 3-D tube-topology calls and inspect a low-x/high-x gallery. |
| Cross-sectional morphology after strut localization | StrutSurf [4] uses micro-CT to automate per-strut diameter, ellipticity, orientation and roughness; the published workflow is strut-specific rather than slice-specific. | Supplies the rationale for keeping cross-sectional/local projections and per-strut tables together. | At a ~4-voxel diameter, ellipse/diameter claims are not defensible on this real scan; use the higher-resolution simulated data for those classes. |
| CAD/volume correlation in a strut-local domain | de Pastre, Quinsat & Lartigue [5] propose virtual volume correlation for AM lattice-strut shape defects directly from XCT data. | Supports local coordinate transfer/registration refinement as the next step after this screen. | It is not a 2-D detector and does not remove the measured 0--3 voxel drift by itself; local alignment must be validated before rates are reported. |

The implemented transformed-space experiment is recorded in `outputs/method_comparison/strut_aligned_2d_projection/`.  It is a **maximum projection**, not a sum projection: sum would preserve attenuation burden but makes a single gap less visible when neighbouring material overlaps, while max makes the presence screen easy to read but can conceal a gap.  Neither is treated as an independent defect-rate measurement.
